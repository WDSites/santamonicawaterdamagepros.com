The fastest way to reach the line is to call (424) 550-5662. It is answered 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays.

What to have ready when you call

  • Your street address and the nearest cross street.
  • What kind of building it is, such as a house, an apartment or a condo, and which floor.
  • Where the water came from, if you know, and whether it has been shut off.
  • Whether the water is clean, such as a supply line, or dirty, such as sewage or storm water.
  • Whether anyone is in danger or whether there is a gas or electrical risk.

You do not need all of this to call. Dispatch will ask what it needs, and you can answer as you shut off the water or move to a safe place.

If water is spreading while you read this

Close the main valve

Stopping the source is the single most useful thing you can do before the crew arrives.

Stay safe near power

Cut power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and reachable. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911 first.

Take photos

Photograph the damage before moving anything. Your insurer will ask for them.

Call the line

Describe what happened and where, and follow the first steps dispatch gives you.

Details that help the dispatcher move quickly

  • The address of the property and the nearest cross street.
  • Where the water came from and whether it has been shut off.
  • Which rooms or floors are wet and roughly how much of each.
  • Whether the water looks clean, gray or contaminated.
  • Whether you own the property, rent it or manage it for someone else.
  • Your insurer name, if you plan to file a claim.

None of this is required to start the call. If you do not know an answer, say so, and dispatch will walk through the next safe step with you.

While you wait for the crew

If it is safe, shut off the water at the main valve and turn off power to affected areas at the panel. Do not stand in water near outlets or appliances. Lift small items and furniture legs off wet floors, and take photos of the damage before anything is moved.

Do not use a household vacuum on standing water. Do not open walls or ceilings that are sagging with water, because the load can fall. If you think the water is contaminated, keep children and pets out of the room.

Which page matches your problem

Standing water in the home points to emergency water removal. Wet materials that need drying point to water mitigation. Fast cleanup after a leak points to water damage cleanup. Rebuilding comes later under water damage repair. Flooding from outside is covered under flood damage restoration, and suspected growth is covered under mold remediation.

The phone line is the only way to reach dispatch. There is no chat, no form and no email inbox monitored for emergencies, so if water is moving, call. For non-urgent questions about a past job or a document, the business email on this page is the place to write.
