A crawl space is the low area under a raised floor. It holds pipes, wiring, insulation and the wood that supports your floor. When water gets in, it soaks all of them and lifts moisture into the rooms above.

Basements are not typical in this part of Los Angeles County. Homes on a raised foundation have a crawl space instead, and that low void is where plumbing, wiring and insulation sit beneath the floor, so it is the usual place to find water under a house here.

How water gets under a Santa Monica home

Plumbing is the main source. A split supply line, a drain that has come apart and a water heater leak can all drop water through the floor. Rain is the second. NOAA climate normals for the Santa Monica Pier station show about 13 inches of rain in a typical year, concentrated from November through March, and heavy storms can push water through vents or along grading.

Coastal areas carry added risk. The 2025 Local Hazard Mitigation Plan update, as reported by the Santa Monica Daily Press, names catastrophic flooding among the city's greatest risks and sea level rise as the costliest long-term threat. Low coastal ground, such as parts of Venice, sits closer to those coastal risks.

Safety steps before you open the access panel

Do not enter standing water or tight spaces alone

Crawl spaces hold wiring, sharp edges and sometimes pests. Never enter water near electrical lines. A tight space can also trap fumes or stale air. Look from the opening with a flashlight, and call instead.

  • Close the main water valve if the source is plumbing.
  • Photograph what you can see from the opening.
  • Do not run a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Keep the access panel clear and note where it is for the crew.

How the crew handles a wet crawl space

  1. 1

    Pump

    Submersible pumps remove standing water, and the crew works in protective gear.

  2. 2

    Remove wet insulation

    Soaked batts and damaged vapor barriers are removed and bagged.

  3. 3

    Dry

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry joists and subfloor, and moisture readings are logged.

  4. 4

    Check and clean

    Surfaces are cleaned, and growth is documented. See mold remediation if growth is present.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference for drying and water categories. The California Residential Code includes an under-floor space section covering ventilation, clearance and access, which is why a crawl space has vents and an access opening. Blocked vents and low spots are part of the checklist.
