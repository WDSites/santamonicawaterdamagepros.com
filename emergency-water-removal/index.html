If water is standing in your home right now, this is an emergency, and the 24/7 line is open. Emergency water removal is the first stage of any wet loss. Pumps and extraction units pull standing water out of the structure before it soaks into drywall, insulation and subfloors.

While you wait for the crew, close the main water valve if you can reach it, keep people and pets out of the wet area and call the line. The sooner the water is out, the fewer materials have to be torn out and replaced.

When standing water is an emergency

Any water you can see pooling on a floor counts. Water does not stay where it lands. It runs under baseboards, into wall cavities and down through floors into the unit or garage below, which is a real risk in the apartment and condo buildings around downtown Santa Monica.

After hours is when many of these calls start. A supply line gives way overnight, a water heater lets go while the house is empty or a storm drives rain through a roof seam. The line answers at those hours, and same-day response is offered when a crew is available.

Stay out of the water near power

If water is touching outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, do not walk into it. Cut power only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911 or the utility before you call the restoration line.

What puts water on the floor in Santa Monica homes

Failed supply line

Braided washer hoses, toilet connections and under-sink lines give way without warning and can run for hours.

Water heater failure

A split tank or a burst fitting releases a full tank of water in a closet or garage.

Roof and window leaks

Wind-driven rain near the coast finds flashing gaps and failed seals, then drips into ceilings.

Drain and sewer backup

A blocked line pushes dirty water up through floor drains and tubs. Treat it as contaminated.

Storms can bring more than a drip. In January 2005 a heavy storm closed the California Incline because of mudslide and flood risk, and flooding briefly shut the McClure Tunnel where the I-10 meets Pacific Coast Highway, according to the Santa Monica Daily Press. If storm water is entering your home, treat it as contaminated until a crew says otherwise.

How the crew removes the water

  1. 1

    Stop the source

    The crew confirms the water is shut off or the leak is isolated.

  2. 2

    Extract

    Submersible pumps and extraction units pull standing water from floors and low spots.

  3. 3

    Map moisture

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera find water behind walls and under floors.

  4. 4

    Hand off to drying

    Air movers and dehumidifiers take over, covered under water mitigation.

Extraction removes bulk water fast, but it does not dry a building. Wet drywall, insulation and subfloor can hold moisture long after the floor looks clear, which is why drying equipment follows the pumps.

Burst pipe water damage

A burst pipe is the classic emergency call. The first job is the shutoff. Many homes have a main valve where the water line enters the building or at the meter, and finding it before you need it saves minutes when you do. If you do not know where it is, look now and note it for everyone in the household.

After the valve is closed, open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to drain the line and reduce further leaking. Photograph the broken pipe and the wet areas, then call the line. A plumber repairs the pipe, and the restoration crew handles the water that came out of it.

In older Pico and Sunset Park homes, original plumbing may still be in service, so ask a plumber to inspect the other lines before walls are closed back up. In a multi-unit building, tell the building manager immediately, because the pipe may serve several units and the water may already be in the unit below.

What affects the price of water removal

  • Water categoryClean, gray and black water are handled and disposed of differently.
  • Depth and areaHow much water stood and how many square feet it reached.
  • Materials affectedCarpet, pad, drywall, hardwood and insulation respond differently to soaking.
  • Equipment daysThe drying equipment placed after extraction and the days it runs.
  • Rebuild scopeAny materials that must be replaced once the structure is dry.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowner and renter policies, while gradual leaks often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep the photos and records the crew provides.
