Mold remediation is the process of containing and removing mold after a moisture problem, then fixing the conditions that fed it. It is a secondary service on this line, and it often follows a leak, a roof problem or a slow drip behind a wall.

The two things to know first are simple. Mold needs moisture, so the water source must be found and fixed. And disturbing mold by scrubbing or demolishing without containment can spread spores through the home. Call before you start tearing out materials.

Do you have dark spots, a musty smell or a spreading ceiling stain?

Most people do not search for remediation first. They notice dark spots in a closet corner, a musty smell in a bathroom, a bubbling paint line on a ceiling or a damp wall behind furniture. Those are the symptoms that bring them here.

If the spot is small and on a hard surface, a basic cleaning may handle it. If it is spreading, sits on drywall or insulation, or came back after cleaning, the moisture is still there and the growth often reaches behind what you can see. Call the line and describe what you found.

What mold remediation involves

  1. 1

    Find the water

    The crew locates the moisture source and confirms it has been stopped.

  2. 2

    Contain

    Plastic barriers and negative air keep spores from moving into clean rooms.

  3. 3

    Remove

    Mold-affected drywall, insulation and porous materials are removed, and surfaces are HEPA vacuumed and cleaned.

  4. 4

    Dry and verify

    The area is dried and checked, and clearance testing is offered when you need written results.

Remediation follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for professional mold remediation. S520 is a published industry standard, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. Cleaning alone, without drying, is not remediation.

Mold testing and inspection

Testing answers specific questions, not general ones. It can confirm whether a spot is mold, show whether airborne spores inside differ from those outside and verify that a cleanup worked. An inspection starts with moisture meters and a visual check of where water traveled.

Clearance testing is done after remediation to confirm the area is ready for normal use. When the testing must satisfy a lender or a buyer, an independent tester who did not do the remediation gives the results more weight. Ask whether the test will be done by someone who is independent of the crew.

Health questions about mold exposure belong to a physician. A restoration crew can tell you what is growing where and how to remove it, but it cannot say how mold affects a specific person.

Mold removal and black mold cleanup

Black mold is a popular name for dark-colored growth. Color does not tell you the type of mold, and the label should not change the plan. Dark growth on a wall or ceiling is handled the same way as any other mold: stop the moisture, contain the area, remove affected materials and clean.

Hard surfaces such as tile, glass and sealed wood can often be cleaned. Porous materials like drywall, carpet and insulation usually have to be removed when mold has grown into them. The crew shows you what is salvageable and what is not before any removal begins.

Mold after water damage

Mold after a leak is the most common mold call. A wall that was wet for several days, carpet pad that was never dried or a ceiling that dripped overnight can all grow mold. The sooner drying starts, the less likely mold follows.

If you had a water loss and the smell never went away, the structure may still be wet. Water mitigation finds hidden moisture with meters and a thermal camera. Water damage cleanup removes affected materials, and the crew will tell you if mold work is needed.

Santa Monica homes near the coast can stay damp in cool, humid weather, and closets, bathrooms and lower-level rooms are the usual trouble spots. A condensation line, a failed window seal or a slow drip under a sink can feed growth for weeks before it is noticed.

  • Size of the areaThe square footage and the number of rooms involved.

Insurance treats mold according to its cause. If it grew from a sudden and accidental water loss, a policy may cover some of the work, while mold from neglect or a slow leak often is not covered. Check your policy or ask your insurer. Ask whether your state licenses mold work before you hire anyone, including this line.
