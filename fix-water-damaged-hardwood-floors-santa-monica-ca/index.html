Fixing Water Damaged Hardwood Floors in Santa Monica, CA

What to do in the first day, how drying works and how to tell whether a floor will be sanded or replaced.

A hardwood floor that gets wet starts changing within hours. Boards absorb moisture, swell and begin to cup, and the earlier drying starts, the better the odds of saving the floor.

This guide covers what to do first, how drying works and how to tell whether a floor will be refinished, repaired or replaced. It applies to solid and engineered wood floors.

What happens when wood gets wet

Wood is hygroscopic, which means it takes on moisture from its surroundings. When water reaches a board, the board swells. If the moisture comes from below, the underside swells more and the edges lift. That lift is called cupping.

Crowning, where the center of the board rises, can follow when floors are sanded before they are fully dry. Cracks and gaps can show up as boards shrink after drying. Each depends on how fast the water was removed.

The first day

  1. Stop the source. Close the valve or contain the leak.
  2. Remove standing water with a mop or wet vacuum if it is clean and away from power.
  3. Lift furniture, rugs and anything that traps moisture on the floor.
  4. Do not use heat to speed drying. Heat can crack boards.
  5. Photograph the floor and the source.
  6. Call the 24/7 line so moisture can be mapped and drying started.

How a crew dries a hardwood floor

  1. 1

    Meter the boards

    Moisture meters read the boards and the subfloor to see how deep the water went.

  2. 2

    Extract

    Surface and trapped water are removed.

  3. 3

    Dry

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run, and mats or panels may pull moisture from the boards.

  4. 4

    Monitor

    Readings are logged until the floor reaches a safe moisture level.

Drying is controlled. Too slow and mold can start. Too fast and the wood can crack. The crew adjusts equipment as readings change.

Solid, engineered and floating floors react differently

Solid hardwood can often be dried, sanded and refinished because the whole board is wood. Engineered flooring has a thin wood layer over a core, and water can make the layers separate. Floating floors have seams that can swell and curl at the edges.

Tell the crew which type you have and, if you know it, the brand and the age. A manufacturer may have moisture limits that affect what can be saved. If standing water is still on the floor, start with emergency water removal and then move to drying.

Do not seal a damp floor under rugs, plastic or a new coat of finish. Trapped moisture is how cupping turns into permanent warping, and it can also feed growth under the boards.

Boards that sit on a wet subfloor need the subfloor dried first. See structural drying for how crews read and dry it.

Santa Monica local note: where the water starts

According to the City of Santa Monica's 2025 water quality report, the city gets up to 75 percent of its water from local groundwater wells and buys the rest from the Metropolitan Water District. A leak from a supply line in your home starts as clean water.

Clean water is the best case for a wood floor. The risk grows with time and with whatever the water crosses on the way. A slow leak under a floor is much worse than a quick spill because the boards stay wet and the subfloor holds moisture.

Ask a plumber to check lines under the floors of older homes in Pico and Sunset Park after a leak. If you are in Venice or Pacific Palisades, the City of Los Angeles water system supplies the same kind of supply-line risk.
