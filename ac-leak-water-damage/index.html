An air conditioner removes water from the air, and that water has to go somewhere. When the drain line clogs, the pan overflows or a coil freezes and thaws, the water leaks into ceilings, closets and walls. Most AC leaks are small and slow, and that is why they last.

Turn the system off at the thermostat. A running unit keeps making water. Then set a bucket under any drip and call.

How common AC leaks are on this coast, and where they happen

Santa Monica is mild. NOAA climate normals for the Santa Monica Pier station show winter lows near 50 degrees and an almost dry summer, so cooling is a seasonal load. Mini-split indoor units, ducted air handlers in closets and attic systems are the usual places for leaks.

Because most heat arrives in late summer and fall, a clogged drain can sit unnoticed through the cool months and then fail at the first hot spell. Condos and apartments add another layer. A ceiling stain in one unit may come from the air handler of the unit above.

Safety steps for the first hour

Power off before you touch anything wet

Air conditioners carry power to the unit. If water is near the air handler or the panel, switch the system off at the thermostat, then at its breaker only if you can reach it on dry ground. Do not stand on a wet ladder or climb into an attic with water on the framing.

  • Turn the system off at the thermostat.
  • Move belongings from under the leak.
  • Photograph the unit, the drain and the damage.
  • Call an HVAC technician to clear the drain, and the restoration crew for the water.

How the crew handles AC water

  1. 1

    Map the water

    A moisture meter and thermal imaging camera show how far water traveled through the ceiling and the wall.

  2. 2

    Dry the cavity

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wet area, and soaked insulation is removed.

  3. 3

    Check for growth

    Condensate water is often not clean. Surfaces are cleaned and checked for early mold.

  4. 4

    Repair

    Drywall, texture and paint are restored once the readings are dry. See water damage repair.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference for drying and water categories. Condensate sits in pans and drain lines where dust and microbial slime collect, so it is usually treated as more than plain clean water. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth.

What changes the cost

Duration

A slow drip over weeks soaks more material than a one-day overflow.

Location

Attic and wall cavities cost more effort to open and close.

Finishes

Textured ceilings and paint matching add repair work.

Growth

Early mold adds a separate scope.

Policies often cover a sudden overflow and often exclude gradual leaks and lack of maintenance. The California Department of Insurance advises keeping records and reporting a loss promptly. The cost of the HVAC repair itself is a separate matter.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Running the system while it is leaking.
  • Pouring bleach into a drain line without advice from an HVAC technician.
  • Painting over a stain before moisture is confirmed dry.
  • Skipping a yearly drain check before the first hot weeks.
