Water damage cleanup is what happens after the water has stopped and the standing water is out. It is also an emergency service in the first 48 hours, because wet debris, soaked carpet and contaminated surfaces start to break down quickly. The 24/7 line is open, and a crew is sent the same day when one is available.

Cleanup is different from drying and from rebuilding. The job here is to remove what cannot be saved, clean and sanitize what can, and leave the space ready for either drying or repair. While you wait, keep people and pets out of any dirty water and avoid moving soaked materials by hand.

Why the first 48 hours matter for cleanup

Wet materials give off moisture and odor, and some of them hold contaminants. Carpet pad, upholstery and cardboard soak up water like a sponge and become a source of smell and bacteria. A crew sorts these out first, before they spread the problem to materials that could have been saved.

In multi-story buildings, a slow cleanup also means water continues to travel to units below. Tell the building manager about the loss and ask neighbors to check ceilings, especially in the apartment buildings near downtown Santa Monica and along Ocean Avenue.

What cleanup includes

Debris and soaked materials

Wet carpet, pad, soft drywall, insulation and contents that cannot be dried in place are removed and bagged.

Surface cleaning

Hard surfaces, framing and floors are cleaned of silt, dirt and residue left by the water.

Sanitizing

Antimicrobial treatment is applied where the water category calls for it.

Deodorizing

Odor sources are removed first, then air and surfaces are treated.

The category of water decides how aggressive the cleanup has to be. Water from a clean supply line is handled differently from gray water out of a washing machine, and both are handled differently from sewage. The crew identifies the category on site and explains what it means for your materials before work starts.

Safety before cleanup

Contaminated water needs protection

If the water came from a sewer, a toilet with solids or a flood, avoid direct contact and keep people and pets away. Wear waterproof gloves and boots if you must enter, wash hands afterward, and leave the cleanup to the crew. Check for electrical hazards before entering any wet room.

Santa Monica's water system serves up to 75 percent local groundwater treated at the Arcadia Water Treatment Plant, according to the city's 2025 water quality report, so a leak from your own plumbing starts as clean water. The risk rises as water sits, soaks through dirty surfaces and picks up what is on the floor.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowner and renter policies, while gradual leaks often are not. Flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and photograph the damage before anything is removed.
