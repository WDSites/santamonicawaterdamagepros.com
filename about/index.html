Santa Monica Water Damage Pros is a 24/7 emergency line for water damage restoration and mold remediation in Santa Monica, Venice and Pacific Palisades, California.

What the line does

When you call, dispatch asks where the water came from, where it is now and whether anyone is in danger. You get safety steps while a licensed and insured restoration crew is arranged. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available.

Everything on this site is built around a phone call. There are no web forms, because water damage does not wait for a form to be answered. You call, you talk to a person and the first instructions are given on the call.

Services covered

The line covers emergency water removal, water mitigation, water damage cleanup, water damage repair and flood damage restoration. Mold remediation is a secondary service that follows leaks and moisture problems.

Restoration work is described using published industry standards. ANSI/IICRC S500 covers water damage restoration and S520 covers mold remediation. The site names them so you know what to ask any restoration company, including the one that is sent to you.

What the line does not do

Fire and smoke cleanup are not part of this site. Basement waterproofing and foundation work are separate trades, and dispatch will say so when that is what you need. The line does not offer equipment rentals and does not publish fixed prices, because a real price depends on the water category, the area, the materials and the drying time.

Why local knowledge matters

Santa Monica is a coastal city with older homes in Pico and Sunset Park, apartment buildings downtown and exposed properties near the pier and the Palisades Park bluffs. Venice has walk streets, canals and low ground. Pacific Palisades has hillsides, canyons and burn-scar runoff. Each has a different water problem, and each has its own page.

The city's drinking water is up to 75 percent local groundwater treated at the Arcadia Water Treatment Plant, according to the City of Santa Monica's 2025 water quality report. Knowing that a failed supply line starts as clean water helps a crew explain what happens next and how fast it can change.

How pricing and insurance are handled

The scope of work and the way the price is set are explained before work starts. Insurance is described in general terms only. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

The crew keeps moisture readings and photos so your insurer can see what was found and what was done. For details on coverage and claim steps, the California Department of Insurance publishes a residential claims guide.

Where the business is based

The business address is 100 Wilshire Blvd, Santa Monica, CA 90401. Service happens at your property, so the fastest way to reach the line is the phone. See the contact page for the address, the email and what to have ready when you call.

How a call becomes a job

The call starts with safety. Dispatch asks whether anyone is in danger, whether electricity is near the water and whether the source has been shut off. Those answers decide what you are told to do while a crew is arranged.

Next comes a short description of the loss: where the water came from, which rooms are wet, what materials are affected and how long the water has been there. A crew uses that picture to bring the right extraction and drying equipment, so the first visit is not wasted.

On site, the crew checks moisture with meters, documents the affected areas with photos and writes a scope of work. The scope explains what will be extracted, dried, removed or cleaned, and how the price is set. You review it before work begins.

What the guides on this site are for

The blog holds fifteen guides on costs, insurance, floors, walls, basements and mold. Each one ends with a local note drawn from city, state or federal sources, so the general advice is tied to Santa Monica rather than copied from a national template.

The guides are meant to help you decide what to ask. They do not diagnose a property from a distance, and they do not replace an on-site inspection by a qualified crew.

If you are comparing options, ask any company the same four questions: what is the scope, how is the price set, what standard does the work follow and who is responsible for each step. Clear answers to those four questions tell you more than any slogan.
