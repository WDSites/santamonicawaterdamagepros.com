Pico is a mixed residential and commercial neighborhood in the middle of Santa Monica, centered on Pico Boulevard. Cleanup here often involves more than one party, such as a landlord, a tenant and a shop owner, so knowing who to call first saves time.

A local ArcGIS storymap on the neighborhood describes its edges as Lincoln Boulevard to the west, Centinela Avenue to the east, Olympic Boulevard to the north and Pico Boulevard to the south, about a mile and a half across. The same source notes that the city GIS outline runs past Lincoln Boulevard into what residents usually call Mid-City. The Pico Neighborhood Association is a city-recognized organization.

How the neighborhood got its shape

The storymap says the area takes its name from Pio Pico, the last governor of Alta California under Mexican rule. It describes the Santa Monica Air Line railroad running from 1909 to 1953, wartime hiring at Douglas Aircraft, and the construction of Interstate 10 in the 1950s and 1960s, which displaced many families. Ishihara Park, named for a Japanese American veteran, was built after residents protested a rail maintenance yard.

The same source describes many homes as California Craftsman houses and calls Pico historically the most affordable and working-class part of Santa Monica. Reference descriptions call the area mixed residential and commercial. A restoration plan has to fit the building in front of you, whether it is a bungalow, a multi-unit building or a shop.

Who to call first

If you rent, tell your landlord or building manager in writing and keep copies, because the source of the water may be in a shared line or in another unit. If you own, a plumber stops the source and the restoration crew handles the water that came out. If you manage a shop, see commercial water damage for what a crew can and cannot do in a commercial space.

Ask your manager or the City of Santa Monica Public Works whether a backup is in the public sewer or in your private line. The answer decides who repairs it. For water from a drain, read sewage backup cleanup before touching anything.

Safety first: protect people before property

Keep people and pets away from water that came from a drain or toilet. Stay out of water near outlets, cords and the panel. Shut off the water supply only if you can reach it safely, and leave and call 911 for gas odors or sparks. Photograph damage from a dry spot.

What the record shows about flooding

No public record reviewed documents repeated flooding in the Pico neighborhood. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified, so check an address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. After a January 2005 storm, city officials said only minor flooding occurred and credited the drainage system. Most calls start with a leak inside the building.

How cleanup and drying proceed

Crews extract standing water, then use moisture meters to find water inside walls and under floors. Wet drywall, carpet and pad that cannot be dried are removed, and the remaining surfaces are cleaned. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure until readings match a dry reference.

The sequence is covered under water damage cleanup, emergency water removal and structural drying. Ceiling stains from an upstairs leak follow the steps under ceiling water damage repair.
