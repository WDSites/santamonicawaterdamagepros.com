Venice was founded in 1905 as a seaside resort, and the homes in it show the century that followed. Water damage restoration here means working around old plumbing, alley access and a low-lying coastal setting. The 24/7 line is open every day of the year.

Venice sits on the coast about 10 feet above sea level, along the Santa Monica city line, and shares its beachfront Ocean Park and Dogtown edge with Santa Monica. Crews dispatched from Santa Monica reach Venice by Main Street, Lincoln Boulevard or Pacific Avenue.

A century of Venice homes leaks in different ways

Venice was an independent city until 1926, when it merged with Los Angeles. Houses built in the early decades of that century sit beside newer infill, and the plumbing behind the walls often belongs to different eras. When a supply line fails, ask a plumber to inspect the lines next to it, not just the one that broke.

Early 1900s multifamily buildings line Pacific Avenue, some with as many as 30 units on one lot. In a building like that, water from one unit can drop through several floors, so tell the building manager at once and ask neighbors to check their ceilings.

The inland walk streets hold about 620 single-family homes that are reached on foot, with car access by rear alleys. That layout matters in an emergency. Tell dispatch whether your home faces a walk street or an alley, because it changes where the crew parks and how equipment is carried in.

When water is spreading in Venice

Call at once if water is moving across a floor, dripping from a ceiling or rising from a drain. Coastal storms and high tides are not the only cause. Pipe failures can flood a block on a clear day, and a Venice home can be reached from several directions in the first hour.

In 2026, two separate pipe failures flooded parts of Venice in one day. One was a pipe owned by a landlord, and the other was a water main break in an alley off Main Street. LADWP crews restored service at both. Who owns the broken pipe decides who repairs it, so ask your building manager or LADWP before you assume the leak is yours.

If water is still spreading

Close the main valve

Find the shutoff for your unit or house and close it. In a multi-unit building, call the manager to shut the building main.

Mind the outlets

Cut power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and reachable. Stay out of water near outlets.

Lift what you can

Raise electronics, rugs and boxes off wet floors, especially in ground-floor beach units.

Keep clear of dirty water

Keep people and pets away from sewage or storm water, then call the 24/7 line.

Low ground, canals and a former lagoon

The Venice Canals were dug to drain marshland, and most were filled in after annexation, though the Venice Canal Historic District remains. The Windward Circle traffic circle sits on the site of a former lagoon, and the Venice Post Office there dates to 1939. Low ground like this means water has less room to drain when it gets in.

Rain has a record of washing Venice Beach itself. In 2010 the county spent $1.6 million to replace 30,000 cubic yards of sand that rainstorms had washed away. To see whether your address sits in a mapped flood zone, check the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask your insurer about flood coverage, which standard homeowner policies usually exclude.
