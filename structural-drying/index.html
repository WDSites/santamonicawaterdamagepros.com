Structural Drying and Dehumidification in Santa Monica, CA

Structural drying removes water from framing, subfloors and walls that look dry on the surface. The 24/7 line scopes the equipment, the readings and the drying goal.

Structural drying is the equipment and measurement side of water damage work. It dries framing, subfloors, wall cavities and masonry, which hold water long after the surface looks dry. Dehumidification then keeps the air from putting that moisture back.

Drying is not guesswork. The crew sets a target moisture content, places equipment and checks readings daily until the target is reached. A fan alone does not dry a structure.

What gets dried and what does not

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference for structural drying and lays out the principles of controlling the room, the air and the materials. Wood framing, plywood subfloor, drywall, plaster, concrete and insulation each dry at different rates, and some materials are cheaper to replace than to dry.

Drying by material
MaterialHow it behaves when wetTypical approach
Wood framing and subfloorAbsorbs slowly, releases slowlyDry in place with air movers and dehumidifiers
DrywallSoaks quickly and weakensDry if lightly wet, remove if saturated
InsulationHolds water against framingUsually removed when wet
Concrete and masonryHolds moisture for a long timeDehumidify and monitor
HardwoodCups and swellsDrying mats and monitoring, or replace

Why drying is a local question

The coast is mild. NOAA climate normals for the Santa Monica Pier station show winter lows near 50 degrees and a dry summer, so building materials rarely get a freeze to shed moisture and rely on equipment. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth.

In a multi-unit building, wet walls and floors cross unit lines. The Census Bureau American Community Survey for 2019 to 2023 puts owner occupancy in Santa Monica at 28.8 percent, so many losses involve a shared wall, a stack of units or a building owner who has to coordinate access.

Safety steps while equipment runs

Respect cords and airflow

Drying equipment runs on household power and makes noise for days. Keep cords out of walkways, never cover a dehumidifier or air mover and keep pets and children away. Do not turn equipment off or move it without calling the crew, because that breaks the drying setup.

  • Keep windows and doors closed so the room stays controlled.
  • Do not stack items against equipment.
  • Empty any collection pan the crew shows you, if there is one.
  • Call if equipment trips a breaker.

How the drying is done

  1. 1

    Set the goal

    The crew measures unaffected materials to set a dry standard for the building.

  2. 2

    Place equipment

    Air movers, low-grain dehumidifiers and drying mats or panels are placed by room and material.

  3. 3

    Monitor

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera are read each visit and recorded.

  4. 4

    Finish

    Equipment comes out once readings reach the goal, and the log is given to you.

If water is still standing, start with emergency water removal. If materials were soaked with dirty water, water damage cleanup covers the clean-out before drying.

Drying after a sudden covered loss is often covered, and your insurer may ask for moisture logs. The California Department of Insurance advises keeping records and reporting a loss promptly. Ask for the daily readings, because they prove when the structure was dry.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Renting a single fan and calling the job done.
  • Closing walls before readings reach the goal.
  • Turning equipment off to save power.
  • Assuming a dry-feeling floor is dry underneath.
