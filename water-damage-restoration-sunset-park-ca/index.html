Sunset Park is the largely residential part of Santa Monica south of Pico Boulevard, with Santa Monica Airport along its southern edge. In a small one-story house, water from one failed line can spread through the hallway and bedrooms before anyone notices, so the first hour counts.

Reference descriptions place Sunset Park between Pico Boulevard and the southern city limits, and east from Lincoln Boulevard to the city line. Friends of Sunset Park is one of the neighborhood organizations the city recognizes. As with the other neighborhoods on this site, the boundaries are approximate and the streets are landmarks only.

Homes built for aircraft workers

Reference descriptions say most Sunset Park houses are small one-story homes from the 1940s, when Douglas Aircraft was hiring for wartime production. The Santa Monica History Museum says the Douglas plant at the airport provided jobs and developed the neighborhood around it. The Los Angeles Conservancy says Douglas became the largest private employer in Santa Monica. Many houses have since been remodeled or rebuilt.

A house date does not show what is inside the walls. Pipes, water heaters and roofs may have been replaced in any decade, and the foundation type varies from house to house. That is why a crew reads your floor and walls with a meter before it chooses equipment, instead of assuming how the house was built.

Water damage in a compact floor plan

With few interior walls and short distances between rooms, water from a failed supply line, a water heater or a washing machine hose can reach several rooms quickly. Flooring, baseboards and the lower part of the drywall take on moisture first. Furniture and boxes on the floor soak it up from underneath.

Drying is planned room by room, with air movers pushing air across wet surfaces and dehumidifiers pulling moisture out of the air. The water mitigation page explains the equipment, and appliance leak cleanup covers the machines that fail most often indoors.

What the record shows about storms here

During a December 2021 storm, the National Weather Service gauge at Santa Monica Airport recorded 1.56 inches of rain. Santa Monica police told the Santa Monica Daily Press the storm caused nothing out of the ordinary in the city. No flooding in Sunset Park was reported in that coverage.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified for this page, so check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Rain can still enter a house through a failed roof section, flashing or a window seal, and those leaks call for a roofer as well as drying. See roof leak water damage.

Safety first: before you touch anything

If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out of it and switch off power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it from dry ground. Close the water shutoff if you can do so safely. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave the house and call 911.

  • Move rugs, shoes and boxes off wet floors and photograph the damage first.
  • Keep children and pets out of any room where the water came from a toilet or drain.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Do not open walls or ceilings yourself if you see dark staining or if the house is older and the material is unknown.

When the water is dirty

Water from a toilet, a floor drain or a backed-up line is contaminated. Carpet, pad and soft drywall that it touched usually have to be removed, and hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized. The steps are laid out under sewage backup cleanup, and a plumber or the City of Santa Monica handles the blocked line.
