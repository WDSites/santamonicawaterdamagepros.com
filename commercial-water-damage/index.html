A water loss in a business is different from a loss in a home. More people are involved, access can be limited and the building may hold several occupants with separate policies. The work itself is extraction, drying and cleaning, and the 24/7 line is open now.

This page covers only what the crew actually does for a commercial space: extract water, dry materials and structure, clean and remove ruined finishes, and document the work. It does not describe particular industries, clients or contract terms.

Where commercial water losses start

Common sources are failed supply lines, sprinkler and fixture leaks, roof leaks, drain backups and overflow from an upper floor. A leak that starts in a restroom or a mechanical room can travel through a ceiling or along a shared wall into a neighboring space.

The Santa Monica Daily Press reported on the city's 2025 Local Hazard Mitigation Plan update, which names catastrophic flooding among the city's greatest risks. In the January 2005 storm, the paper reported flooding that briefly shut the McClure Tunnel where the I-10 meets Pacific Coast Highway. Storm water and drains matter to any business near the coast.

Safety steps for staff and occupants

Clear the area and cut the source

Move people out of the wet area. Close the water source if you know where it is. If water is near electrical equipment, panels or servers, stay out and leave power decisions to a qualified person. If you smell gas or see smoke, evacuate and call 911.

  • Notify the building manager or property owner.
  • Photograph the area before moving anything.
  • Move inventory, files and electronics off wet floors if it is safe.
  • Note which rooms must reopen first.

How the crew handles a commercial loss

  1. 1

    Extract

    Pumps and extraction units remove standing water from floors.

  2. 2

    Map and remove

    Moisture meters and thermal imaging find wet areas. Ruined carpet, ceiling tiles and drywall are removed.

  3. 3

    Dry

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and readings are logged.

  4. 4

    Report

    Photos, readings and a scope are given to you for your insurer.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference for water damage restoration, and it applies to commercial buildings as much as homes. For wet walls and framing, see structural drying and water mitigation.

What changes the cost

Access and hours

Work around occupied space or off-hours work changes the plan.

Water type

Clean, gray or contaminated water is handled differently.

Commercial property policies differ from the homeowner policies described in the California Department of Insurance residential claims guide, which covers homes only. Read your own policy, ask your agent about business interruption terms and keep every record.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Waiting to notify the building owner.
  • Reopening carpeted areas before readings are dry.
  • Letting wet ceiling tile hang in place.
  • Skipping a yearly check of roof drains and restroom lines.
