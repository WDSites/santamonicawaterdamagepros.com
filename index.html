Water damage restoration in Santa Monica

What water damage restoration includes

Water damage restoration is the work of stopping the source, removing standing water, drying the building materials and repairing whatever could not be saved.

In Santa Monica that work usually starts with a call about a burst supply line, a failed water heater, a roof leak after a coastal storm or a sewer line backing up into a lower unit. Each one needs a different first move, and the emergency line is staffed to sort that out in the first minute of the call.

The crew that arrives is licensed and insured and works to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. S500 is a published industry standard, and you can ask any restoration company whether its crews follow it before you agree to work.

Mold work belongs in the same conversation when moisture sits too long. Mold needs moisture to grow, so the leak must be found and the structure dried before any mold work will hold. Mold remediation is handled as a secondary service on the same line.

Emergency steps

Before the crew arrives

The first hour decides how far the water travels. These four steps are safe for most homeowners to take while the 24/7 line is arranging a crew.

Stop the source

Close the main water valve or the valve at the fixture. If the water is coming from a neighbor above you, tell the building manager at once.

Mind the power

Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. Never step into standing water near outlets or appliances.

Protect what you can

Lift small valuables, rugs and electronics off wet floors. Pull cushions and boxes away from walls.

Stay out of dirty water

Keep people and pets out of sewage or storm water, and wash hands after any contact.

Electrical and gas risk come first

If water has reached an electrical panel, you smell gas or you see sparks, leave the area and call 911 or the utility before you call the restoration line. A wet floor can be dried later. A shock cannot be undone.

Take photos and short videos of the damage before anything is moved. Your insurer will ask for them, and the crew adds moisture readings to the same record.

A crew assigns the category on site, and a category can move up if water sits. Category 1 water left for days is treated differently from the same water caught within hours, which is one more reason to call early.

Local conditions

Water damage across Santa Monica, neighborhood by neighborhood

South of Wilshire, around the Third Street Promenade and Santa Monica Place, many buildings stack homes and shops over one another. A leak in one unit can reach the unit below, so tell the building manager early and ask neighbors to check their ceilings.

Ocean Park, in the southwest corner of the city with its Main Street shops, and the area around the Santa Monica Pier are exposed to ocean weather. Wind-driven rain can find gaps around windows and roof edges, which is why a stained ceiling after a storm deserves a close look.

Pico is a historic neighborhood split by the Santa Monica Freeway (I-10). Older buildings may still carry original plumbing, so once the water is stopped it is worth asking a plumber to check the lines before the walls are closed.

Sunset Park, next to Santa Monica Airport, has small single-story homes built in the 1940s for aircraft workers. In a small floor plan one failed supply line can reach most rooms quickly, which makes the first hour count.

Santa Monica's tap water comes from up to 75 percent local groundwater treated at the Arcadia Water Treatment Plant, with the remainder purchased from the Metropolitan Water District, according to the city's 2025 water quality report. A failed supply line therefore starts as clean water, and the clock on that category starts the moment the leak does.

Cost factors

What affects the cost of water damage restoration

No honest number exists until someone sees the damage. These are the factors that move a restoration price the most.

  • Water categoryClean, gray and black water call for different cleaning and disposal steps.
  • Affected areaThe square footage and the number of rooms the water reached.
  • Materials involvedCarpet, pad, drywall, insulation, hardwood and cabinetry each dry or fail differently.
  • Drying days and equipmentThe number of air movers and dehumidifiers and how many days they run.
  • Rebuild scopeWhat must be replaced and finished after drying is complete.

Insurance and your claim

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered by a homeowner or renter policy. Slow leaks and neglect often are not. Flood damage is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage, so check your policy or ask your insurer.

The California Department of Insurance advises policyholders to report a loss promptly, take reasonable steps to protect the property and keep records of calls and letters. Its residential claims guide explains the process.
