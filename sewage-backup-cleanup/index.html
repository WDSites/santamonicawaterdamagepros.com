A sewage backup pushes wastewater up through floor drains, tubs and toilets. It is contaminated, so the cleanup differs from a clean water leak. The 24/7 line is open now.

Keep people and pets out of the area, avoid contact with the water and wash with soap after any exposure. Do not run water or flush toilets while the line is backed up, because it adds to the flow.

Why sewer lines back up

Most backups come from blockage. Tree roots, grease, wipes and collapsed older pipe stop flow in a private lateral, and water has nowhere to go but up the lowest drain. During heavy storms, flows into the system rise as well.

The coast gets most of its rain in a few winter months. NOAA climate normals for the Santa Monica Pier station show about 13 inches a year, with February the wettest month. After the January 2005 storm, city officials credited the drainage system and sloping terrain for limited damage, according to the Santa Monica Daily Press, but older lines can still be overloaded.

In apartment buildings the blockage is often in a shared line. The Census Bureau American Community Survey for 2019 to 2023 puts owner occupancy at 28.8 percent, so most Santa Monica households rent and the building owner usually controls the sewer line. Tell the manager at once.

Safety steps for sewage

Treat sewage as contaminated

Do not wade through it or clean it with household tools. Keep children, pets and anyone with a weak immune system away. Wear rubber boots and gloves only for a short move, and wash your hands with soap and clean water afterward.

  • Stop using sinks, toilets and showers.
  • Call a plumber to clear the line, and the restoration crew for cleanup.
  • Do not run fans that spread contaminated air.
  • Photograph the damage and notify the building manager if you rent.

How the crew handles sewage

  1. 1

    Contain and extract

    Pumps and extraction units remove the water, and the crew works in protective gear.

  2. 2

    Remove porous materials

    Carpet, pad, insulation and drywall that soaked are removed and bagged because they cannot be reliably cleaned.

  3. 3

    Clean and sanitize

    Hard surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products.

  4. 4

    Dry

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and readings are logged.

ANSI/IICRC S500 classes sewage as Category 3, the most contaminated class. That class decides what is removed instead of dried in place. The same logic is covered under flood damage restoration for storm water that enters from outside.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Cleaning sewage with a mop and bleach.
  • Keeping carpet and pad that soaked in sewage.
  • Using water in the building until the line is cleared.
  • Flushing wipes and grease down drains.

Ask a plumber about a camera inspection if backups recur. If water reached wall cavities, water mitigation covers drying them.
